# Policy Brief: Reaching Young People Who Are Not in Employment, Education or Training (NEET)

*Sample brief prepared as a writing example. Figures are illustrative; a client version would cite the latest official data.*

## Key messages
- NEET is not one group. It includes short-term jobseekers, young carers, discouraged young people and people with disabilities, and each needs a different response.
- Young women are over-represented in long-term inactivity, mostly because of care responsibilities rather than unemployment.
- Programmes work best when employment services, education and family/social services share data and a single point of contact.

## 1. Why it matters
Long spells outside work and education in early adulthood are linked to lower lifetime earnings, poorer health and lower trust in institutions (the "scarring" effect, see Eurofound, 2012; OECD, 2016). The cost falls on both individuals and public budgets.

## 2. Who are the NEET?
International frameworks (Eurofound, 2016) separate seven sub-groups: re-entrants, short-term unemployed, long-term unemployed, unavailable due to illness/disability, unavailable due to family responsibilities, discouraged workers and "other inactive". In most middle-income countries the **family-responsibility** and **discouraged** groups are the largest and the hardest to reach through employment offices alone.

## 3. What works
| Approach | Evidence | Practical lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Youth Guarantee-type offers within 4 months | Positive where services are well staffed (European Commission, 2020) | Speed matters, but so does quality of the offer |
| Outreach through schools, NGOs and family services | Reaches inactive young people missed by registers | Use trusted local actors |
| Childcare + training packages | Raise participation of young mothers | Remove the barrier, not just offer the course |
| Early-warning systems for school dropout | Promising, but fairness risks | Audit models for bias before use |

## 4. Recommendations
1. **Segment before you target:** publish NEET figures by sub-group, sex and region every year.
2. **One-stop contact:** a named case worker who coordinates employment, education and social support.
3. **Make care compatible with training:** pair programmes with childcare places and flexible hours.
4. **Measure outcomes at 6 and 18 months**, not just enrolment.

## Limitations
This sample uses secondary literature only and does not evaluate a specific national programme.
